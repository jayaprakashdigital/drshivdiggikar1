Newborn Jaundice Bangalore — Causes, Signs & When to Act

👨‍⚕️ Dr. Shivashankar Diggikar, FRCPCH 📅 2025 ⏱ 8 min read 📍 Medicover Whitefield, Bangalore

Jaundice in newborns is one of the most common reasons a family ends up back at the hospital in the first week of life. While jaundice is often harmless and resolves on its own, there are specific situations where it requires prompt medical attention.

What Causes Jaundice in Newborns?

Jaundice occurs when bilirubin — a yellow pigment produced by the breakdown of red blood cells — builds up in the blood faster than the baby's liver can process it.

Signs of Newborn Jaundice — What to Look For

Jaundice progresses in a predictable pattern — it starts on the face and forehead and spreads downwards. A useful rule: the further down the body the yellow colour has spread, the higher the bilirubin level is likely to be.

To check: press gently on the skin with a finger. When you release, if the area looks yellow, jaundice is likely. In darker-skinned babies, look at the whites of the eyes and inside the mouth — more reliable indicators.

⚠️ When Is Jaundice an Emergency? — See a Doctor Immediately
  • Jaundice appears within the first 24 hours of life
  • Yellow discolouration spreads rapidly to the abdomen, legs, and feet
  • Baby is extremely sleepy and difficult to wake for feeds
  • Baby is arching backwards or has a high-pitched, unusual cry
  • Stools are pale/white or urine is dark yellow/orange
  • Jaundice persists beyond 3 weeks in a full-term baby

How Is Newborn Jaundice Treated?

  • Phototherapy (Light Treatment) — Blue-spectrum LED lights break down bilirubin in the skin. Painless, effective, and completely safe. The baby's eyes are covered with small protective patches.
  • Enhanced Breastfeeding — Increasing feeding frequency to 10–12 times in 24 hours often resolves breastfeeding jaundice without phototherapy.
  • Intravenous Immunoglobulin (IVIG) — Used specifically in haemolytic jaundice (blood group incompatibility).
  • Exchange Transfusion — Reserved for very severe cases. The baby's blood is gradually replaced with donor blood to rapidly lower bilirubin.

Can Jaundice Cause Brain Damage?

Untreated severe jaundice can cross into the brain and cause kernicterus — a condition causing permanent neurological damage. However, this outcome is very rare in a setting where jaundice is properly monitored and treated. When identified and treated early, the risk of brain damage is essentially eliminated.

"The purpose of phototherapy is precisely to prevent bilirubin from ever reaching damaging levels. Early identification is everything." — Dr. Diggikar
